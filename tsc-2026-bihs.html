BIHS at The Science of Consciousness 2026

BIHS returns to The Science of Consciousness this October as part of a two-part workshop examining some of the most challenging questions at the edges of consciousness research.

The session brings together researchers studying out-of-body experiences, past-life memories, remote perception, and the relationship between consciousness and the brain.

Sunday Morning Workshop

Research at the Edge of Consciousness: OBEs, Past Life Memories and Altered States of Consciousness

Part 1: 8:30–10:15 am
Part 2: 10:45 am–12:30 pm
Chair: Kunal Mooley
Location: Dockside Room

Near-death experiences, out-of-body experiences, and reports of past-life memories raise a provocative scientific question: does consciousness depend entirely upon the brain?

The workshop approaches that question through several different lines of investigation—from veridical perception during out-of-body experiences and systematically studied childhood memories to neurobiological research and experiments involving remote perception.

Why This Conversation Matters

Much contemporary consciousness research begins with the relationship between subjective experience and neural activity. Experiences such as OBEs and reported past-life memories introduce another question: whether all features of consciousness can be adequately explained by brain processes alone.

For BIHS, participation in conversations such as these creates an opportunity to engage difficult questions about consciousness with researchers who are approaching them through empirical study, philosophy, and interdisciplinary dialogue.

Continuing the Dialogue

BIHS has participated in The Science of Consciousness in previous years, bringing questions informed by consciousness studies and Indian philosophical traditions into conversation with the wider international research community.

The San Diego workshop continues that dialogue through a focused examination of experiences that remain difficult to accommodate within conventional theories of mind.
