Graduate Education Initiative

From Vision to Institution

Śrīla Prabhupāda wanted the philosophical and spiritual knowledge of the Bhāgavata tradition to engage seriously with the modern world.

Could an academically credible institution of higher education help fulfill that aspiration?

This page provides context for the questions presented in the meeting. It describes a proposed graduate education initiative—not an approved or accredited degree program. The purpose is to support thoughtful discussion of mission, feasibility, responsibilities, and next steps.

01 · Our purpose
Could higher education become a lasting expression of Śrīla Prabhupāda's vision?

The founding aspiration of serious engagement between the Bhāgavata tradition and the modern intellectual world can be pursued through many activities: research, publications, conferences, dialogue, and education. Graduate education is proposed as one possible enduring institutional pillar—not the only legitimate path.

Principle: Academic authorization and accreditation would be means to serve an educational mission, not ends in themselves.
02 · Why graduate education?
We must not only contribute to today's conversations. We must educate those who will carry them forward.

Develop future scholars

Structured, sustained graduate study can cultivate critical reasoning, research skills, and intellectual independence.

Build continuity

Faculty mentorship, learning outcomes, and academic processes can sustain educational work beyond individual events and leaders.

Deepen scholarship

Graduate seminars can connect existing research, philosophical reflection, and future scholarly contributions.

Complement existing work

Conferences, retreats, archives, publications, and public programs can contribute to—and benefit from—graduate education.

03 · Our distinctive contribution
How do we know? What is reality? How should we live?

The proposed program would draw intellectual inspiration from the Bhāgavata tradition while examining serious alternatives through open inquiry. Students' academic work should be evaluated for evidence, argumentation, understanding, and scholarly quality—not religious agreement.

Program structure under development: one proposed 36-quarter-unit master's degree with three concentrations. Curriculum and regulatory requirements remain subject to review.

04 · Our academic foundation
We are not starting from zero. We are bringing together an existing community of scholars.

BIHS has intellectual heritage, existing educational activity, and relationships with scholars across philosophy, science, consciousness studies, and related fields. These are meaningful starting assets.

Important distinction: Scholars in our network are not necessarily appointed faculty. Before launch, the Institute must document qualifications, obtain formal commitments, assign academic responsibilities, and establish adequate administrative and student support capacity.

A separate verified faculty roster should identify degrees, disciplinary expertise, proposed teaching roles, and the status of each person's commitment.

05 · The pathway
Licensing allows us to begin. Academic quality earns us credibility. Accreditation can help sustain it.
1 · Florida authorizationPrepare and submit an appropriate application to the Florida Commission for Independent Education (CIE), with required institutional, academic, and financial documentation.
2 · Responsible operationAfter authorization, establish and demonstrate successful academic instruction, student support, assessment, and financial controls.
3 · Accreditation readinessPlan toward eventual institutional accreditation, subject to the chosen accreditor's criteria, eligibility, and independent review.

State licensure is not institutional accreditation. A September 2027 inaugural cohort is a planning target, contingent on authorization, financing, staffing, and readiness. No approval or accreditation is implied.

A vision becomes an institution only when we commit the resources to sustain it.

Cost assumptions to verify

Earlier planning used approximately $2,500 per faculty-taught course, $5,000 annually for Canvas and $500 monthly for librarian support. Course count, library content licenses, faculty workload and incremental technology costs must be confirmed.

Separate funding evidence

Distinguish expressions of interest, pledges, matching conditions, unrestricted gifts actually received and projected tuition. No conditional donation should be treated as cash available for expenditure.

Operational sustainability

Establish yearly faculty and administrative costs, student-support requirements, tuition policy, realistic paid enrollment, scholarships and reserves. A start-up allocation alone cannot show that the degree can sustain itself.

Board checkpoint

Reconcile the draft scenario with CIE Form 606, the approved catalog, independent financial evidence and a conservative cash-flow forecast before any binding launch commitment.

Critical distinction: A proposed $150,000 use-of-funds plan is not proof of $150,000 available. The Board should review both the cost to launch and the cost to operate over the full student program.
07 · The Board's decision
Are we prepared to build an institution that will educate generations beyond our own?

The immediate choice need not be an unconditional commitment to launch a degree. It can be a commitment to evaluate this path rigorously and establish clear decision points.

Strategic alignmentDetermine whether graduate education should be a long-term BIHS priority.
Defined preparationAuthorize a clear next stage of planning, with responsibilities, scope, deadlines, and budget controls.
Decision checkpointReview completed academic, licensing, financial, and staffing evidence before undertaking major commitments.
The aspiration is enduring; the method must be responsible. Build with intellectual openness, academic rigor, and financial discipline.
